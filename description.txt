# Smart City Power Grid: System Description & Architecture

This document provides a comprehensive overview of the Smart City Power Grid, its 10 microservices (grouped into 6 functional categories), the Machine Learning (ML) observability pipeline, and the autonomous fault remediation engine.

---

## 1. System Vision
The system is designed to provide high-fidelity observability into a simulated urban power grid. Its primary objective is to maintain a 100% "Healthy" grid status by detecting electrical, thermal, and distribution anomalies and executing corrective actions (remediations) in under 15 seconds.

---

## 2. The 6 Microservice Categories

### I. Grid Controller (SCADA Hub)
The **Grid Controller** is the central nervous system of the grid. It acts as the SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) server.
- **Use Cases**:
    - **Telemetry Aggregation**: Polls every 5s to compute the global "System Status" (Healthy/Degraded/Critical).
    - **State Management**: Maintains the authoritative record of `active_alerts` and `remediation_log`.
    - **Dashboard API**: Provides the React frontend with a unified `/metrics/summary` endpoint.
    - **Simulation Control**: Manages the global `is_simulating` flag to enable/pause the entire grid logic.
    - **Service Orchestration**: Issues `RESET` commands to all services when a simulation session ends.

### II. Transformer Node (Infrastructure)
This service manages 16 virtual transformers that step down high-voltage transmission to distribution-level power.
- **Use Cases**:
    - **Thermal Monitoring**: Sensors track core temperature (°C) and oil levels.
    - **Load Sensing**: Reports the `load_percent` of each individual transformer.
    - **Cooling Control**: Manages mechanical cooling fans which can be activated to mitigate thermal stress.
    - **Failure Modes**: Simulates "Cooling Fan Failure" or "Thermal Overload" due to high ambient heat or local demand spikes.

### III. Zone Distribution (Sector Loads)
There are 5 zone services (North, South, East, West, Central) representing urban consumption sectors.
- **Use Cases**:
    - **Sector Demand**: Simulates thousands of homes and businesses; reports `current_load_mw`.
    - **Feeder Health**: Internal sensors monitor the "Feeders" (main lines) for overloads.
    - **Capacitor Bank Control**: Can activate local capacitor banks to stabilize power factors.
    - **Load Shedding**: Supports rapid de-energizing of non-essential sectors during grid-wide emergencies.

### IV. Load Balancer (Optimization)
The **Load Balancer** ensures that no single transformer or zone becomes a bottleneck.
- **Use Cases**:
    - **Path Optimization**: Routes power across multiple transmission paths to minimize resistance and heat.
    - **Cross-Zone Redistribution**: Dynamically shifts capacity from a zone with surplus (e.g., Industrial at night) to one with deficit (e.g., Residential).
    - **Dynamic phase balancing**: Balances the load across Phase A, B, and C to prevent neutral current spikes.

### V. Voltage Regulator (Security & Stability)
Focuses on the "Quality" of power, ensuring it stays within the strict 210V–244V window.
- **Use Cases**:
    - **Tap Changer Management**: Requests physical adjustments to transformer taps to raise/lower voltage.
    - **Harmonic Suppression**: Uses digital filters to reduce THD (Total Harmonic Distortion) caused by heavy electronics.
    - **Reactive Power Compensation**: Balances kVAR to keep the **Power Factor (PF)** above 0.90, preventing "Reactive Power Collapse".

### VI. Fault Detection (Safety & Waveform Analysis)
The "Security" layer that monitors low-level electrical signatures.
- **Use Cases**:
    - **Earth Fault Detection**: Monitors "Residual Current" (mA); detects if electricity is leaking to the ground.
    - **Arc Fault Detection**: Analyzes waveforms for high-frequency "Arc Signatures" that precede electrical fires.
    - **Insulation Breakdown**: Tracks the megohm (MΩ) resistance of underground cables.
    - **Rapid Isolation**: Triggers safety interrupts that de-energize a specific feeder in <1s.

## 3. How the System Works (Data Flow)

1.  **Telemetry Loop**: Every service generates metric data (Normal vs anomalous). The `Grid Controller` polls these every 5s, while the `ML Anomaly Detector` polls every 2s for high-speed response.
2.  **Simulation Layer**: `run_app.py` runs a background thread that injects randomized failures (e.g., "Voltage Spike", "Current Surge") if the simulation is toggled "ON" in the dashboard.
3.  **Inference Pipeline**: 
    -   **Detection**: An **Isolation Forest** model looks at 35+ combined features to see if the current state is an "Outlier" (Anomaly).
    -   **Classification**: A **Random Forest Classifier** then identifies the "Root Cause" (e.g., "Is this a heat issue or a voltage issue?").

---

## 4. How We Resolve Problems (Autonomous Remediation)

When a fault is classified, the **Remediation Engine** follows a "Plan-Act-Verify" sequence:

1.  **Action Selection**: Maps the fault type to a specific sequence of API calls.
    -   *Example (Thermal Overload)*: `ACTIVATE_COOLING` -> `REBALANCE_LOAD` -> `ALERT_MAINTENANCE`.
2.  **Execution**: Calls the target microservice endpoints (e.g., `/cooling-fan/start` on the Transformer service).
3.  **Verification**: 
    -   The engine waits for a 3s "settling period".
    -   It re-polls the metrics to see if the fault is still present.
4.  **Safety Forced-Restart**: If the fault persists for more than 6 seconds, the engine issues a `RESTART` command to the physical component (Transformer or Zone) as a last-resort safety measure.
5.  **Logging**: Every single decision, action, and outcome is logged to the `remediation-log` for human audit.

---

## 5. Performance Targets (SLA)
- **Mean Time To Detect (MTTD)**: < 5 seconds.
- **Mean Time To Resolve (MTTR)**: < 15 seconds (Targeted < 10s).
- **SLA Compliance**: Measured based on the 15s window from injection to "Healthy" status.
